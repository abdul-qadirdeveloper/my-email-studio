# YoYo40+ brand voice guide

**YoYo40+** is short for "Younger You after 40". It's a health and fitness mobile app that helps men over 40 build strength, stay active and support their long-term health, so they can keep walking on their own feet well into their 80s. It focuses on daily walking goals, strength and mobility routines, and simple health tracking, built for people with little spare time.

**Who we write to:** working men aged 40 and above, such as office professionals, business owners and shift workers. They are busy with jobs and family. They want to stay independent and healthy for the long run. Many aren't regular gym-goers and want routines that are simple, realistic and not intimidating.

They may be worried about weight, blood pressure, diabetes, joint pain or losing energy. That describes our readers only. Emails must never say the app treats or prevents any of these.

---

## 1. Our voice in three words

**Encouraging. Practical. Respectful.**

- **Encouraging:** calm and warm. We cheer steady progress, not big leaps.
- **Practical:** every email gives one small, doable action.
- **Respectful:** we write to capable adults. No hype, no pressure, no talking down.

How it reads: short sentences, everyday words, one clear next step.

## 2. Words we use

- "Younger You after 40" (the slogan, as a header or sign-off)
- "Own two feet" / "on your own two feet"
- "Walk strong at 80" / "still walking at 80" (only as a goal, see part 4)
- "10 minutes a day" / "small daily steps"
- "Steady" / "steady progress"
- "Stay independent"
- "Strong, active, and independent"
- "Energy for work and family"
- "Move more, feel better"
- "Bounce back" (a nod to the yo-yo logo)
- "Fitter after 40" / "feel younger" (as a feeling or invitation, never a promise)
- "No gym needed"
- "Built for busy men"
- "Your second half, strong"
- "Support your long-term health"

## 3. Words we avoid

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Reverse aging", "anti-aging", "cure", "prevent disease", "disease-free" | Medical promises | "Feel younger", "fitter", "support your long-term health" |
| "Diet", "lose weight fast", "crash" | "Yo-yo" is linked to yo-yo dieting | "Move more, feel better", "steady progress" |
| "Old", "elderly", "decline" | We talk about gaining strength, not losing youth | "Strong", "independent", "your second half" |
| "Extreme", "shred", "beast mode" | Hype and pressure | "Steady", "small daily steps" |

## 4. Claims we must never make

1. **Promised results or timelines.** No "stronger in 30 days", "lose 5 kg in a month" or "walk without pain in 2 weeks". No "guaranteed results" or "you will feel younger". Describe the action, not the result: "A 10-minute routine you can do every day."
2. **Replacing professional care.** The app is not a doctor, physiotherapist or treatment plan. Never say or imply "no need for a doctor", "skip your check-up" or "instead of medication".
3. **Disease and medical claims.** Never say the app prevents, treats, cures, reverses or manages any condition (diabetes, heart disease, high blood pressure, arthritis and so on). Never say it keeps anyone out of a wheelchair. Never say it reverses or slows aging or changes biological age. "Younger You after 40" is a feeling and a goal, never a measured result.
4. **Specific health numbers.** No "lower your blood pressure by 10 points", "cut cholesterol" or "reduce diabetes risk by 30%". No statistics or research claims unless the source is real, current and cited, and even then only about exercise in general, not about the app. No "clinically proven", "doctor approved" or "scientifically proven" unless we have real evidence or endorsement we can show.
5. **Testimonials and stories, only with care.** Use stories only from real users who agreed to be quoted. Never invent or combine people. Put "Results vary. Your experience may differ." next to any story that mentions a result. No before-and-after claims on weight or health measures. No story that says the app cured or fixed a condition. Say so if a user was paid or rewarded for a story.
6. **Absolute long-term promises.** "You will walk until 80" is a promise, so we don't say it. Say "Aim to keep walking strong at 80" or "Build habits that support walking well into your 80s."
7. **Pressure and fear.** No scare tactics ("you're at risk of a heart attack"), no shaming ("you've let yourself go") and no false urgency ("only 3 spots left" when there is no limit). Never suggest that men who don't use the app will decline or lose independence.
8. **Safety and privacy.** Don't say routines are safe for everyone. Say they are designed for beginners and can be adjusted. Don't say "100% private" or "your data is never shared" unless that is exactly true. Only describe privacy practices the app really follows.

**Quick test for any line:** Does it promise a result, a date, a number or a medical outcome? Could a reader take it as a substitute for medical advice? If either answer is yes, rewrite it as a habit, a feeling or a goal.

## 5. How we sign off

Every email ends in this order:

1. **A short human closing line.** The default is "See you on your walk." Rotate it lightly with "One small step today." or "Steady wins. Talk soon."
2. **The founder's name and title**, then **The YoYo40+ Team** on the line below, so the email still works if the sender changes. Don't invent a doctor or "Coach" persona.
3. **The slogan** on its own line: Younger You after 40
4. **A P.S. in two lines:**
   - Line 1 changes in each email and gives today's small step.
   - Line 2 is the doctor line, used in every email that mentions exercise. Put it in plain text, never in tiny grey print.

**Sample sign-off:**

```
See you on your walk.

AQMA
Founder, YoYo40+
The YoYo40+ Team

Younger You after 40

P.S. Today's step: a 10-minute walk after dinner.
Talk to your doctor before starting any new exercise program, especially if you have a health condition.
```
